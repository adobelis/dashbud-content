## Key insight: the Lovable anti-pattern
At Success Academy, CEO assumed "AI can handle it" and gave everyone access to Lovable. People built their own data reports — results were chaos, numbers Alyssa was pretty sure were wrong. She had started implementing centralized data sources (the right direction) but found the experience frustrating given the CEO's expectations. Her conclusion: the end product of doing it right would have been a set of data and contextual resources that let Lovable act like Dashbud. **Dashbud is what you get when you do AI + data correctly from the start.**

Her prior experience at Popular Bank was the opposite end: a massive centralization project that gave strategic visibility but never got to self-service distribution across teams.

## ICP refinement
- Best target: companies at a scale where they *might* consider hiring a dedicated data person. Dashbud replaces that hire + avoids a centralization project + provides instant self-service.
- Multi-site businesses past 4-5 locations — at that point you can't be hands-on with operations.
- True small biz (single storefront) is too small: you'd have to sell the concept of data analytics itself.

## Buyer persona
- Head of data (operational or analytical person, possibly strategic).
- Ops leads — they have diverse/legacy data sources (mix of old and new platforms, spreadsheets) managing process, budgets, facilities.
- Top IT person (CTO/CIO, possibly fractional) collaborates with ops/finance/marketing stakeholders on setup.
