## Take-home points
* Alyssa love the website and the scroll-demo. 
* Having implemented analytics successfully at a large bank and also bailed out of a role at a company where her CEO wanted to just "let data handle it," she gets what the product does and how it solves problems at two ends of the spectrum:
  1. Implementing data analytics is difficult for many reasons: data cleaning and normalization is critical, and centralization is a whole project. At Popular Bank she went from a non-analytics situaiton where people had uneven access to various raw data sources and very little visibility into customer activity generally to having a marketing and customer tracking data lake. Huge process, and never went the next step of distributing access to all teams; but the business had strategic visibility at least.
  2. "Putting it all in AI" is a mess. At Success Academy, her CEO thought AI could just "handle" it, so they gave everyone access to Lovable. Alyssa thinks Lovable is a beautiful product, but when people started to create data-driven reporting for themselves, the result was chaos. Poeple would show her numbers that she was pretty sure were wrong. She had started implementing centralized data sources, which is the right direction to go, but she found the experience frustrating -- given her boss's assumption that "AI can do it," easy to see why. Essentially, the end product would have been a set of data and contextual resources to let Lovable act like Dashbud. 
* Her main question was along the lines of "who's it for," getting more specificity and identifying use cases to solve. She was thinking that some storefront businesses might be good candidates. My concern with the true "small biz" market (one shop e.g.) is that I don't want to have to sell data analytics or visibility, and I think at a certain small scale the value isn't as obvious. Once you get to having 4-5 storefronts, a true multi-site business, then you simply can't be as hands-on in your understanding of your operations. I think companies that are at a scale where they *might* consider hiring a dedicated data person present the clearest value-add: Dashbud gives your ops, finance, and strategic leads access to data without having to hire a full-time data person to implement an analytics system, write reports, etc. Your top IT person (CTO, CIO -- possibly fractional) can collaborate with your top ops/finance/marketing stakeholders to figure out what data goes into the system, what data sources go together, what business rules need to be modeled. Not only can you avoid a hire, but you also get instant self-service, avoid a centralization project, and avoid queues for report-writing.
* We discussed who is the best person to sell to in a mid-scale company (we're not going to sell to Popular or TIAA for a while). She thought the head of data, preferably an operational or analytical but possibly a strategic person. Also possibly ops leads -- typically they have diverse/legacy data sources: a mix of old and new platforms, spreadsheets, etc., to manage process, budgets, facilities, etc.
