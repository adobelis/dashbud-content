## Key GTM advice

### 1. Just start reaching out — there's no shortcut to the first 50 conversations
- At Dashbud's stage and ACV, it's literally Arthur reaching out via LinkedIn and email
- Target: CEOs and operators of mid-market companies with old ERPs
- Build a list of ~100 targets using Claude (e.g., warehouses that have been around 40+ years, likely on legacy ERP, right size)
- Customize each email — don't blast. At this volume, every message should be personal
- 1 in 20 response rate is normal and fine at this stage

### 2. Lead with pain, not product
- "Don't lead with Dashbud. Lead with the pain."
- Use language that shows you understand their world: "Does your ERP let you do a price-volume-mix analysis?" or "How many exports are you running from your database every week?"
- The framing of "blue screen ERP" is evocative — use insider language that makes the reader think "this person gets it"
- Arthur's reaction: this is the right approach — "language targeting around pain points, subtle pain points, things that make you sound knowledgeable, empathetic"

### 3. Start producing content NOW — even if it's bad
- Fernando's strongest recommendation. Referenced a successful entrepreneur friend (Israeli guy, previously introduced to Arthur ~2 years ago, had a good conversation) who automated content production: an agent watches internal Slack/conversations, produces multiple articles per day, publishes to blog and LinkedIn
- SEO/AEO has a ~6-month lag before your domain gets treated as a content source. Clock doesn't start until you start publishing
- "Even if it's shitty content" — just start. The lag is the bottleneck, not quality
- Suggested: use Dashbud's own customer conversations (anonymized) as source material. "You're going to be doing these interviews. Record them. Distill into blog posts."
- Arthur agreed but noted reluctance about Twitter ("cesspool") while acknowledging LinkedIn is good

### 4. Conferences are high-leverage for early founders
- At this stage, conferences are the most efficient way to get many conversations in one day
- Best play: partner with ModMed at their conference booth (free or cheap, capitalize on their brand)
- Even without a booth, just attending and talking to people is "the most efficient way to founder-sell"
- The argument against conferences ("takes time from building") doesn't apply when you're still founder-selling

### 5. Think about product-led growth
- Fernando's DashFox experience: they built a mechanism where customers' own email contacts became leads (K-factor of 0.6-0.7, not viral but dramatically lowered CAC — 100% of top-of-funnel came from automated referrals, zero website conversion needed)
- Challenge for Dashbud: no obvious viral loop, but Fernando urged Arthur to think about what in the product could make it spread (Dropbox/Slack model — usage creates exposure)
- Not immediately actionable but worth keeping in mind
