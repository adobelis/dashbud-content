# Trillium Creek Dermatology

**Closed-lost 2026-09-21.** The first organic inbound, and the most instructive loss so far.

Dermatology practice (tcohio.com), ModMed EMA customer. Scott also knows ModMed's CEO.

## What happened

Demo 8/19. Then a POC: Arthur rebuilt 10+ reports from their August ModMed exports — appointment and production tables. Michelle declined on 9/21, choosing to "use ChatGPT instead." Arthur and Scott sent a win-back email; no response as of 2026-10-08.

**They never logged into the app.** Every bit of value they saw was a report Arthur delivered to them. They never encountered the semantic model, the governance, or the shared definitions that make the ChatGPT comparison invalid.

## Proof point retained

The POC proved that the two ModMed EMA tables driving a derm practice's monthly reporting ingest easily into Dashbud. That is reusable evidence for every future ModMed or specialty-practice prospect, independent of this loss. The ModMed partnership angle is unaffected.

## Two lessons

**1. POC delivery mode matters more than POC quality.** The work was delivered *for* them rather than *in* the product, so they only ever saw output. From where they sat, output looks replaceable by ChatGPT — because output alone is. Future POCs should hand over app access.

**2. The build-it-themselves signal.** Michelle may have been motivated by personal interest in learning to build this herself. That is a principal-agent problem, not a feature objection: the company wants reliable reporting, the person wants the skill and the credit. A vendor gives the company what it wants while taking away what the person wants — which is why no product argument wins and why the win-back got silence.

Now recorded as a qualification disqualifier in `outreach/sales-nav-targeting-criteria.md`.
