# LinkedIn Launch Strategy — Founder Announcement

## What we planned in September 2025

A year ago we developed a content strategy built around Arthur staying behind the curtain:

- **Blog post** ("The Lost Promise of BI") published under "The Dashbud Team" byline on the company page
- **LinkedIn company post** — a ~350-word teaser hitting the ad hoc reporting problem, link to full article in comments
- **Personal share** — Arthur reshares from his personal profile as a consultant "working with" Dashbud: *"I've been working with the Dashbud team for a few months and am excited about what we're building."*
- **Rationale for staying hidden:** Arthur wanted to save the "new role announcement" for maximum impact when the app launched. He hadn't listed Dashbud as an employer on LinkedIn.

The strategy was: establish Dashbud as a serious voice first, reveal Arthur's role later. The LPBI post was meant to be the first in a content series, with follow-up pain point deep dives.

That plan was put on hold. The LPBI post is live, but no LinkedIn announcement happened, no content series followed, and Arthur hasn't posted on LinkedIn in roughly two years.

## What's changed

| Then (Sep 2025) | Now (Sep 2026) |
|---|---|
| No customers, no proof points | Real sales cycle (TC), real product usage |
| Product not ready for prime time | Live product, active engineering velocity |

## Updated approach

### The announcement is not a product launch

The old plan treated content as a slow build toward a reveal. That made sense when the product wasn't ready. Now the product exists and works. The announcement doesn't need to build suspense — it needs to establish Arthur as someone with a point of view worth following.

### Proposed format: one LinkedIn post, personal profile

No teaser/company-page/reshare dance. Arthur posts directly from his personal profile as co-founder.

**Structure:**
1. **What I've been building** (2-3 sentences) — name the company, what it does, link to site. Don't be coy.
2. **Why** (the bulk of the post) — not the LPBI argument again, but the conviction behind it. What Arthur has seen across his career that made this feel necessary. The "data wonk" experience — the person who ends up pulling exports and building reports because they can.
3. **What we've learned** (2-3 sentences) — a nod to the fact that the product is live and working with real companies. The TC experience without naming TC. Something like: the hardest thing isn't building the tool, it's convincing people that "good enough" with ChatGPT isn't actually good enough when your reporting needs to be trusted.
4. **What's next** — content is coming (blog series, the vibe-code mirage piece, etc.). An invitation to connect, not a sales pitch.

**Tone:** Confident, direct, slightly informal. Not a press release. Not a humble-brag. Arthur has been building something for a year and a half and it's time to talk about it.

**Length:** 300-400 words. LinkedIn posts over 500 words lose most readers before the fold.

### Follow-up content (first 2 weeks)

1. **Day 1:** Founder announcement (above)
2. **Day 3-5:** Reshare LPBI from company page with a short personal note — "I wrote this a year ago. The thesis has held up."
3. **Day 7-10:** First original post — either "The Vibe-Code Mirage" as a short LinkedIn piece, or a shorter version of one of the blog queue ideas
4. **Day 14:** Second original post

This gives Arthur's network time to absorb the announcement before hitting them with content. The LPBI reshare bridges old and new — it's already indexed, already credible, and now has Arthur's name on it.

### What to skip

- No "I'm excited to announce" opener. Everyone does this. Just start.
- No timeline ("after 18 months of..."). Arthur doesn't want to emphasize how long this has been in progress.
- No implication of a large team, but no need to say "it's just two of us" either. Let people assume what they want.
- No hashtags unless LinkedIn's algorithm has changed its mind about them (it hasn't).

## Appendix: September 2025 original plan (from conversation archives)

### Content series strategy (Sep 8, 2025)

The plan was a multi-post content series to re-introduce Dashbud with new messaging and a new focus on business functionality over "gee-whiz" AI. Target: Tier 3 companies — mid-market, data-rich but analytics-poor, $10M-$100M revenue, legacy ERPs, no data team.

**Audience:**
- Primary: leadership in Tier 3 companies (VPs of Finance, Ops, Marketing)
- Secondary: the spreadsheet jockeys / informal data champions

**Series structure:**
1. Introduction post: "The Lost Promise of BI" (problem space)
2. Pain point series: deep dives into specific Tier 3 frustrations
3. Solution glimpses: how AI + design thinking can solve these

### Blog post (LPBI) — final structure

**Title:** "The Lost Promise of Business Intelligence"
**Subtitle/snippet:** "Can AI finally deliver accessible analytics to every business user?"
**Byline:** The Dashbud Team
**Lead image:** Data maze concept — trick maze with dead ends (Data Silos, Spreadsheet Chaos, Analytics Bottleneck, Manual Reporting, Poor Governance) and a glowing mountain peak ("Business Insights") with an AI bridge bypassing the maze.

**Sections:**
1. **The Original Promise** — BI promised facts over hunches; tools arrived, budgets approved, but the fundamental challenge remains
2. **But Ad Hoc Reporting Persists** — sales managers rebuilding Excel analyses, finance stitching exports, sharp minds wasting time; causes: incomplete centralization, expert-focused interfaces, siloed SaaS reporting; added: sharing/visibility problem
3. **Centralization Presents Pitfalls / The Analytics Team Dilemma** — centralized BI creates new bottlenecks; analytics teams stretched thin between standardized dashboards nobody wants and ticket queues; acknowledged real progress but fundamental challenge persists
4. **AI Creates New Possibilities** — separating data governance from analysis; AI tackles both sides: natural language queries for users, semantic mapping assistance for the data foundation; "a well-governed self-service model may finally be within reach"
5. **The Path Forward** — Dashbud introduction; content series preview; "The promise of business intelligence was never wrong. We're here to help deliver it."

### LinkedIn distribution plan (Sep 15, 2025)

**Step 1: Company page post (~350 words)**

Teaser focused on the ad hoc reporting problem. Different title from the blog (SEO reasons — LinkedIn's domain authority could outrank the blog). Proposed titles included:
- "Why Are Teams Still Rebuilding Reports After 20 Years of BI?"
- "The Analytics Paradox: More Data, Same Manual Reporting"

Draft excerpt:
> Some years ago, Business Intelligence platforms made a bold promise: connect your data, build dashboards, and everyone would make decisions based on facts instead of hunches.
>
> The tools arrived. Budgets were approved. Implementation proceeded.
>
> So why is your sales manager still rebuilding the same analysis in Excel every month?

Ended with: *"Link in comments for the full analysis of why BI keeps failing—and what's finally changing."*

**Step 2: Personal share from Arthur**

Arthur's personal reshare (as a consultant, not revealing co-founder role):

> "I've been working with the Dashbud team for a few months and am excited about what we're building. This piece captures the impetus for the project and some of the promise of what AI can bring to BI."

**Step 3: Follow-up content series**

Pain point deep dives, bi-weekly cadence. Never materialized.

### Key decisions from that process

- **No external links in LinkedIn posts** — algorithm penalty, rabbit hole risk
- **Link in comments** rather than in the post body
- **Blog publishes first** — establishes canonical source for SEO
- **Subtitle as question** ("Can AI...?") rather than a statement — invites exploration
- **Tone:** thoughtful, problem-focused, not salesy; "constructively critical without being negative"
- **Arthur stayed hidden** — saved the role reveal for app launch; published as "The Dashbud Team"
