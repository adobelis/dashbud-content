# Downstream: The Skipped Step

_Status: OUTLINE DRAFT, 2026-11-03. Section: downstream (new). Series post 1 of 5. Author: Arthur Dobelis._

**Subtitle:** The frontier labs are building the model. The value is in what happens after.

**Snippet:** Every "let the model handle it" proposal skips the same step: the point where someone with a stake decides what matters. That step is where the business value of AI lives, and it's open territory.

**Topics:** AI, Business Intelligence, Downstream

## 1. Open on a concrete skip

Google's sleep-style memory, the labs' most ambitious answer to "what should the model remember." The architecture is years out, but the more interesting problem is the premise: it asks the model to decide what's worth keeping. That's not a technical gap. It's a step being skipped.

*Choice: this hook is timely and specific, but it dates. Alternative is to open on section 2 directly and use the memory example inside section 3.*

## 2. Name the step

Every "let the model handle it" proposal, memory, agents, business apps, skips the same thing: the point where someone with a stake decides what matters.

- What to remember.
- Which definition of margin is right.
- Who may see which rows.
- Whether this is the number we report to the board.

The model can execute any of those decisions. It can't make them, because mattering belongs to the person who answers for the outcome, not to the content.

## 3. Why the labs skip it, without blame

Their incentive is generality: one model for everyone has to be agnostic about any one business. Prioritization is local, political, and changes when the CFO changes. It's the one thing that doesn't scale, so their architectures route around it.

The result is systems that are superb at the part after the decision and silent on the decision itself. Structure, not competence.

## 4. Define downstream

The model is upstream. Downstream is everything that happens after the token comes out, and specifically the places where the skipped step gets taken: scope, definitions, lineage, where the software runs.

In ML the word means the tasks a model is adapted to. Close enough; practitioners will hear it.

## 5. Why that's good news for independents

The step needs proximity to the customer, not scale. A lab can't ship your company's definitions. Someone who sits with your finance team for a week can. That's a structural opening, and it's wide.

## 6. Where we live

One paragraph. Dashbud is a downstream product for one job, analytics: the AI writes the query and never sees a row, definitions are shared and governed, same question gives the same answer. Said once, then dropped. Weigh the argument accordingly.

## 7. Credit upstream

None of this exists without the frontier models, and the labs' coding agents are the best thing that has happened to downstream builders. The critique is of a skipped step, not of the people building the thing before it.
