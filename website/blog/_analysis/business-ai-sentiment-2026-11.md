# AI sentiment gauge — November 2026

_Read as of 2026-11-03. Purpose: where the typical-to-sophisticated business reader is on AI, so strategic content lands in the right zone. Survey figures came through search aggregators and were date-checked on 2026-11-03; verify against the originals before citing any of them in a post. Dates are month or quarter where the day isn't known._

**One line:** the reader uses AI daily, has stopped being amazed by it, can't show it on the P&L, and is now watching the financing with a raised eyebrow. Not hostile. Tired, and newly suspicious of the money.

---

### Personal use is universal and real

- 2026-10-29 — Deloitte, State of AI in the Enterprise (n=3,235 leaders): 81% report faster decisions and execution with AI.
- 2026-08 — McKinsey, The State of AI 2026 (n=1,719): eight in ten say AI improved their own productivity.
- 2026-10-13 — Wharton / GBK Collective, "The Adaptive Advantage" (n≈1,400 employees at US companies of 1,000+): 62% of managers and above use AI daily, up from 46% a year earlier.
- Implication: nobody needs convincing that the tools work for them personally.

### Organizational value is the sore spot

- 2026-08 — McKinsey: 37% attribute any EBIT impact to AI, flat from 2025; 6% qualify as "AI high performers."
- 2026-10-29 — Deloitte: 25% have moved 40% or more of pilots to production; 74% wanted AI to grow revenue, 20% have seen it.
- 2026-10-13 — Wharton / GBK: managers describing themselves as excited about AI down 8 points year over year, even as daily use rose 16. Prasanna Tambe, via Fortune: "The amazement is kind of wearing off a little bit."
- 2026-Q1 (Teneo annual CEO survey; confirm issue date) — 68% of CEOs doubling down on AI investment in 2026 though most projects aren't yet profitable; 61% of US CEOs call transformation fatigue a growing concern.
- 2024-12 — EY AI Pulse (n=500 US senior leaders): 50% report declining company-wide enthusiasm for AI. **Two years old**; keep only as a baseline showing the fatigue signal predates 2026.
- 2026-Q2 (reported in an April 2026 trends roundup; original source not identified) — 29% of workers, 44% of Gen Z, say they work around the company's official AI rollout.

### The financing story has crossed over into mainstream business conversation

- 2026-10-26 — Anthropic files its S-1.
- 2026-10-27 — CNBC: investors split, "speculative fiction" vs. "generational moment." Roger McNamee: "a very dangerous thing for investors." Fortune, same day: the IPO as "a referendum on AI"; New Constructs: "the most ridiculous IPO of 2026"; Morningstar bullish on lab moats.
- 2026-10-31 — CNBC: valuation talk slipped from about $2T to $1.5T within a week; Microsoft, Google, and Amazon earnings that week added to jitters about the buildout.
- 2026-10-28 — Jon Stewart hosts Ed Zitron on The Weekly Show; 1.3M views within a week. The tell that the bubble argument is now dinner-party material.
- 2026-08 — Altman: there may be an AI bubble. 2026-10 — Bezos at Italian Tech Week warns of overexuberance. 2025-12 (NYT DealBook Summit; confirm) — Amodei on rivals "YOLOing" on spend.

### The general public has turned

- 2026-06 fieldwork, reported 2026-08 — Pew: 52% more concerned than excited about AI, up from 37% in 2021; 9% more excited than concerned. Under-30s: 55% more concerned, a first.
- 2026-09-17 — Pew (global): more people expect AI to cause job loss than growth. US: 71% expect AI to shrink employment over two decades, up from 64% in 2024; 5% expect more jobs.
- 2026-07-28 — Bentley-Gallup: "AI does more harm than good" at 39%, up from 31% in 2025, after two years of improving attitudes. Frequent AI users are twice as likely to fear displacement.
- 2026-09-15 (fieldwork Aug 3–24) — Gallup Work and Education survey: 27% of US workers worry technology could soon make their jobs obsolete, a record since 2017 and up seven points in a year; 34% among ages 18–44.

### What this suggests for our content

Tentative. The Downstream frame is a first version and these implications should be read as hypotheses to test against the next few pieces, not conclusions.

- Don't sell excitement. The reader has spent theirs. Sober, specific, a little wry, and honest about costs will stand out.
- Don't pick a side on the bubble. Dashbud's position holds either way: if the labs are overbuilt, value still has to land somewhere specific; if they aren't, same. Say it once.
- The gap between personal productivity and company payoff is the live question in the surveys. It's also close to what the Downstream series is circling. Worth writing toward, without leaning on the series' vocabulary until it's earned.
- The cost of the 10x (intensification, the treadmill, fewer juniors trained) is underwritten in business media and matches the public mood. Likely a differentiator.
