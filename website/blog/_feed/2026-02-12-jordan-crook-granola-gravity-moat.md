# Granola's Big Bet on Gravity — Jordan Crook, Betaworks

## Summary

Granola (Betaworks portfolio, meeting notes) launched an open MCP server, "an intentional hole in the wall, where information and data can be passed back and forth." The piece argues this is the right kind of moat for the application layer. "Gravity" is earned loyalty through usefulness, as against "walls" (data silos, proprietary formats). "The moat is emotion; the moat is appreciation; the moat is learned behaviors burned into the neurons of human brains." Users create their own leaving costs by building the product into their workflows; the "Recipe" (customizable workflows, e.g. "/what did I miss?") inspires the most loyalty.

Against foundation models: raw capability is "commoditizing rapidly"; app-layer defensibility is personalization and context, "semantic intelligence into the protocol layer." Broader claim: the next two decades favor products that "hand the keys to the kingdom back to users"; cites Hugging Face, Sandbar, Plastic Labs as neutral platforms rather than extractive gatekeepers. "Giving users freedom is precisely what will make them stay."

## Relevance to Downstream

Post 3: a worked example of a garden with a gate. Granola exposing itself to other people's models through a protocol is the same move as Dashbud's agent API, and this piece supplies the investor-side rationale for it. Post 4: "semantic intelligence into the protocol layer" is close to "UX for AI"; what the product offers an arriving agent is understanding, not just data.

Where it stops short: the openness argument is about consumer loyalty. For business data, the gate also has to enforce who may see what. Gravity plus governance is the Dashbud version; worth saying with attribution.
