# Deep Dive: Memory + AI — Jordan Crook, Betaworks

## Summary

Argues that the leverage point in AI memory is curation and agency: who or what decides what memories are stored, retrieved, and promoted. Memory architecture shapes how a product reasons, personalizes, and works for more than one user.

Three dimensions of memory: as community (collective, perspective-based), as identity (what we promote to long-term storage shapes decisions), as complex reasoning (bridging System 1 and System 2).

Three architectures, each a Betaworks company:
- **Social ledger (Granola):** the transcript is the immutable shared source; per-user summaries are generated from it. "Granola understands that the same meeting... should generate a different set of notes on a per-user basis."
- **Moleskine (Sandbar):** users curate explicitly, symbolic storage and tags rather than embeddings; the system is "an intelligent librarian" that doesn't interpret.
- **Cognitive partner (Opponent Systems):** symbolic graph of facts and relationships for reasoning without saturating the context window. "Memory for static retrieval is a known problem... it actually needs to reconstruct a mental map."

Prediction: a few methods will remain "tried and true"; the differentiation will be the curator.

## Relevance to Downstream

Direct support for post 2, from an investor who has looked at the memory market closely. Her conclusion is the skipped step applied to memory: the hard part is who decides what matters. She reaches it through consumer and personal-productivity products; Downstream reaches it through business data. Same conclusion, different route, which makes it worth citing rather than just agreeing with.

The gap in her piece, and the opening for ours: all three architectures are personal or small-group memory. None addresses organizational memory, what a company has agreed is true and who may change it. The social-ledger model (shared immutable source, per-user views) is the closest analogue to a governed semantic model and could be named as such.
