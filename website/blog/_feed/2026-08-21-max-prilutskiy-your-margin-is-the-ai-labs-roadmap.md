# Your Margin Is the AI Labs' Roadmap — Max Prilutskiy

## Summary

Starts from the steamroll threat (Altman, 2024: companies that "assume the model is not going to get better... we're going to steamroll you") and Bezos's "your margin is my opportunity," then argues the standard advice, "don't be a wrapper," is wrong. Two species look identical on day one:

- **Wrappers that compound.** Cursor, Harvey, Devin, Lovable, Perplexity. Customers pay for the system of work around the model, not the tokens; the model is swapped underneath without anyone noticing.
- **Token resellers.** Product is model output plus markup; roadmap is the provider's with a delay. Jasper, the PDF-chat tools erased by one DevDay keynote, Zams ("the application layer went from differentiated, to crowded, to indistinguishable").

**The token reseller test:** "If tokens were free tomorrow, would your customers still pay you?"

What builds the durable asset: "Not better prompts. Customer conversations. The quality bar in every real domain, what 'good' means to a law firm, a dev team, a hospital, isn't in any training set." Two prices move in opposite directions: fixed-capability price falls relentlessly; frontier price never does. Strategy: start on the frontier to discover, then move to commodity models once you can name your quality bar. "The model is fungible underneath; the system of work is not."

## Relevance to Downstream

Strong. It reads as the disagreement (the steamroll thesis) and resolves into agreement: the durable thing is the customer's definition of "good," collected one conversation at a time, which no lab can ship. That is the skipped step stated by a founder who started from the other side. Post 1 can cite the token reseller test as the practitioner's version of "would this still matter if the model were ten times smarter"; post 5 can borrow the two-price observation.

Where it stops short of us: his durable asset is the founder's knowledge of the customer. Ours is the customer's own agreed definitions, written down where the model reads them. His version lives in the vendor; ours lives in the customer's workspace. Worth one sentence of distinction.

Several figures (80% of wrappers fail, 65% churn in 90 days) are repeated from content-farm sources. Don't cite those. The Altman quote and the a16z Harvey line are fine with attribution.

## Quotable (verbatim)

- "If tokens were free tomorrow, would your customers still pay you?"
- "The model is fungible underneath; the system of work is not."
- "Your margin is the model provider's roadmap, right up until the moment you build something that isn't made of tokens. After that, their roadmap is your margin."
