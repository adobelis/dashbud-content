# Who Captures the Value in AI? — Jo Clodic

## Summary

An investor's map of the stack (infrastructure, models, tooling, applications) with a bull and bear case at each layer. Three competing views on where value accrues: models commoditise and applications win; models eat applications; it depends which applications. Lands on the third: "Those embedded in high-stakes, data-intensive workflows and regulated industries accumulate moats naturally; those that merely repackage general-purpose model outputs (so-called wrappers) do not."

Vertical moats listed as proprietary data, workflow depth, customer proximity, switching costs, regulatory requirements. The bear case for verticals is honest: the labs have the strongest incentive to enter the highest-margin verticals, and the window may be narrowing. Also notes the labs' "advisor model" routing pattern, the early-2026 SaaS multiple compression, and that pricing power follows the current bottleneck, which moves.

Altman's founder heuristic, as reported: if the next model release strengthens your product you're probably defensible; if it risks replacing it, you're probably a wrapper.

## Relevance to Downstream

Useful as the balanced reference: it states the steamroll case and the app-layer case fairly and doesn't pretend to know. Post 1 can cite it for the historical pattern (utilities didn't capture electricity's value; factories did) and for the "it depends which applications" framing, which is compatible with the skipped step without being it. The piece never asks *why* vertical applications hold moats; it lists the moats. The skipped step is the why. That's the opening.

## Quotable (verbatim)

- "the layer that creates the most value is rarely the one that captures it."
- "The right frame is therefore not 'infrastructure vs. applications' but: where is the bottleneck today, which companies are anticipating it, and which ones are building moats fast enough to matter when it moves?"
