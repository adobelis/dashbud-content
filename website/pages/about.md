# About Dashbud

## The short version

Dashbud is an AI-powered analytics platform built by Evolytix, a software company that has spent years building custom data solutions for mid-market companies. We got tired of rebuilding the same infrastructure for every client and decided to productize it.

---

## Why we built it

Before Dashbud, we built custom software. Data warehouses, reporting platforms, EMR systems — one client at a time, each with their own data, their own mess, their own reporting needs. Consulting pharmacies managing patient records across hundreds of facilities. Spirits distributors consolidating sales data from dozens of sources. Veterinary companies with 200+ locations that needed a single financial view.

The pattern was always the same: the client had plenty of data, spread across systems that weren't designed to talk to each other. Getting useful reports out meant either expensive custom development or hours of manual work in Excel. The companies that could afford to hire us got a good solution. Everyone else kept wrangling spreadsheets.

We kept thinking: this shouldn't require a custom engagement every time. The problems are the same. Connect the data. Clean it. Model the business rules. Let people ask questions. The only thing that changes is the data itself.

AI made it possible to build that as a product. Not because AI replaces the analyst — it doesn't. But because AI can do the foundational work that used to require weeks of configuration: understanding schemas, mapping business terms to database columns, writing correct queries from plain-language questions. That work used to be the expensive part. Now it's a conversation.

So we built Dashbud.

## What we believe

### Business data has come a long way, but access is still uneven

Twenty years of investment in data infrastructure has produced real results. Many organizations have clean shared resources, embedded analysts, and automated pipelines. But for most mid-market companies, the data landscape is still bottlenecked. Getting answers means filing a ticket, getting special access, or spending hours pulling exports and cleaning them in Excel. The data exists; the friction is in reaching it.

### Reliable analytics requires a system, not just an agent

An AI agent can write a script to pull from an API and answer a question or deliver a report. But reliable analytics requires more: connected data sources, shared business rules, managed access. Managed self-service — centralized data, federated analysis — works. AI makes it affordable for organizations that could never justify the cost before.

### AI should be applied from the bottom up

AI's most reliable role in analytics is encoding data semantics and business rules, then applying that knowledge to answer questions with accurate, sourced analysis. These two levels deliver immediate, verifiable impact. Higher levels — synthetic reports, autonomous analysis, decision-making — are real possibilities, but each requires more oversight and carries more risk. Start where the value is certain.

### Privacy must be encoded in architecture, not policy

If AI never touches your raw data, you don't need to trust it not to leak or train on it. The agent serves as a translation layer: business questions in, correct queries out, based on metadata and business rules. Higher-level AI roles that process data directly may be valuable, but they shouldn't be mixed into your core data access infrastructure.
