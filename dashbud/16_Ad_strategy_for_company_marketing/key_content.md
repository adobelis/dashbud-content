 Looking at Dashbud's positioning and target market, I'd recommend a multi-channel strategy that emphasizes education and problem-solving rather than traditional product promotion. Here's what I think would work well:

## Content-Led Strategy

Your blog post "The Lost Promise of BI" shows you understand the pain points perfectly. I'd build on this with:

**Educational content series** - "Why Your BI Tool Isn't Working" case studies, "5-Minute Analytics" tutorials, comparison guides between traditional BI and AI-powered approaches. This positions you as thought leaders rather than just another vendor.

**Problem-first targeting** - Instead of advertising "AI analytics platform," focus on specific pain points: "Stop waiting weeks for simple reports," "End spreadsheet chaos," "Get answers in plain English, not SQL."

## Targeted Digital Advertising

**LinkedIn for B2B decision makers:**
- Target operations managers, finance leads, founders at 50-500 person companies
- Focus on industries with multiple systems (healthcare, retail, professional services)
- Use video ads showing the 30-second experience: question → instant chart

**Google Ads for intent-based searches:**
- Target "alternative to [major BI tool]," "simple business intelligence," "reporting tool for small business"
- Landing pages that immediately demonstrate the plain English query experience

## Strategic Partnerships

Given your background with Evolytix and focus on companies with legacy systems:
- Partner with ERP/CRM consultants who see reporting gaps daily
- Collaborate with industry-specific software vendors who lack strong analytics
- Work with managed service providers serving multi-location businesses

The key is leading with the problem (fragmented, slow, technical BI) rather than the solution. Your audience likely doesn't wake up wanting "AI analytics" - they wake up frustrated they can't get simple answers from their data.
