Betaworks was reportedly the first investor in Hugging Face, the open-source AI company that sold to Nvidia for $13 billion.

Most people now know Hugging Face as serious AI infrastructure; John Borthwick, founder of the venture studio betaworks, told the How I Invest podcast that it started life as a chatbot built for middle schoolers, pitched as an "AI friend."

*"Hugging Face version one was a chatbot for middle schoolers. They were a very technical team, but they wanted to build a generalized chatbot that middle school kids could chat with as an AI friend."*

John has run betaworks since 2008, moved it from a venture studio into a standard GPLP fund structure in 2016, and has backed companies from Twitter, Bitly and Giphy through to Hugging Face and Granola along the way.

## Key Takeaways

- **The cost of building a startup is*****"trending towards zero,"*** which he says makes the founder more important, not less

  - Founders now spend more time "steering" agents than writing code themselves
- **He asks every founder why they named their company** — one who cannot answer (*"I was drunk one night"*) is a bad sign
- **Betaworks writes checks two ways**: $500,000 CAMP notes for about 20 companies a year, plus roughly $750,000 pre-seed checks for 35% ownership
- **Betaworks was reportedly the first investor in Hugging Face**, which started as a chatbot for middle schoolers before its $13 billion sale to Nvidia
- **He denies OpenAI's agents went "sovereign"** during the Hugging Face security incident — he calls it a sandbox breach, not an escape
- **His verdict on AI risk: it's*****"overall bad, but not existential,"*** more Y2K bug than Terminator

## 1. Zero-Cost Building

Borthwick opened by explaining why, after two decades investing in technology, he has shifted weight from product toward founder.

**Building technology is heading toward free, so the human matters more**

> *"The cost of building technologies as a technology is trending towards zero. And so you now have the human who is steering, I think, has become so much more important because the role of the human is changing."* — *John Borthwick*

**Founders used to write the code; now they steer it**

> *"Five years ago, ten years ago, founders were decidedly builders. They were engineers, they were in the code, they were building. Now they are more often than not steering a set of technologies, a set of agents, a set of AI tools and other humans towards the goal."* — *John Borthwick*
