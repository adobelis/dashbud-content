AI has a PR problem. The people building at the frontier are doing a pretty bad job explaining why everyone else should be excited about it. I'll admit that those of us investing in it have some work to do here, too. I can talk about the economic opportunity for hours. But someone who's worried about their job probably wants to know what any of that means for them. Personally, I get excited about what will happen when you put these tools back in the hands of smart, capable people. For example, a lawyer could get help with work they've always had to do manually. As a result, they can take on more clients or build a company that would previously have required hiring a lot of people. A mechanic could tie an AI agent to smart glasses to make better decisions while repairing someone's car, to both do it faster and more efficiently, but also to see what other problems lie ahead for the customer. Even a restaurant owner can use it to market better. Something that used to cost hundreds of thousands of dollars now costs pennies to run a full marketing program for your small business. It's pretty easy for me to see the benefit because I'm using these products constantly. But I also understand the anxiety this creates for everyone who is wondering what the future looks like. We should spend a lot more time helping other people understand what they could do with these capabilities.
